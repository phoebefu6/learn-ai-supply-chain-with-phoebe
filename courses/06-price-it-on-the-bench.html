Real world

The strongest public forecasting evidence, the M5 competition, reports the winner 22.4 percent better than the best benchmark on Walmart data, and 415 of 5,507 teams (7.5 percent) beating that benchmark at all. A 30 percent cut is a bigger claim than the improvement the winner reported there, on a different error measure and other people's data. It may still be true for yours. Tonight shows what it is worth if it is, and what it is worth if it is not.

Part 1 · covers what a forecast-error cut is worth

From one percent of error to pounds, in five lines

5 min live

A better forecast is worth nothing on its own. It is worth something when it moves stock: less safety stock for the same service, or better service for the same stock. Both paths run through the same formula you used in session 2, and both end in a number finance recognises.

Self-studyCash is once, the licence is every year3 min▶

Released safety stock is working capital: it comes back once, as cash, when you stop replenishing it. The licence is an annual cost. Comparing a one-off release with a recurring fee on the same line is the most common error in these cases, in both directions. The simulator keeps them apart: cash released on one meter, net value a year on another. How finance teams build the full return calculation, with discounting and risk, is taught in AI + Finance: the ROI business case ↗.

Part 2 · covers the vendor claim against the bench

The claim pays, the measurement does not, and the break-even sits between

5 min live

Run the same Northwick inputs with three error cuts: the vendor's 30 percent, the 2.7 percent the learned model achieved over the best spreadsheet method on 45 real SKUs, and the cut where the tool exactly pays for itself. The break-even is the number to take into the room.

LiveBreak-even: the number to negotiate3 min▶

At Northwick's constructed settings (4,000 SKUs, 95 percent cycle service, 4-week lead time, 25 percent holding rate, £250,000 a year), the simulator finds the tool breaks even at a 12.4 percent cut in forecast error across every SKU. Below that it costs more than it saves; above it, it pays.

That turns a sales conversation into a test. You do not need to know whether the vendor's 30 percent is true. You need to know whether their tool beats your best spreadsheet method by more than 12.4 percent on your own held-out weeks. If they will not run that pilot, that is information too.

The break-even moves with every lever. Restrict the tool to the cells where it wins and the break-even rises to 22.9 percent, because the same licence is spread over fewer SKUs. A longer lead time or a higher service target lowers it, because there is more stock for the cut to release.

Self-studyBank it as cash, or bank it as service3 min▶

A smaller error can buy two different things. Cash: keep the 95 percent service target and hold less safety stock. Service: keep today's stock and let the service level rise. On the "Bank it as service" rung, the measured cut on the cells where the learned model wins lifts cycle service to 95.3 percent and fill rate to 99.0 percent (from 98.9), and the margin no longer lost is worth more than the cash path would release. It still does not clear the licence.

Decide which one you are buying before the pilot starts. A vendor reporting "service up" and finance expecting "stock down" will both be disappointed by the same result.

Part 3 · covers what override habits do to the case

An upward habit parks the savings back on the shelf

4 min live

Session 5 measured what a blanket upward override does to accuracy. Here is what it does to cash. Under an order-up-to policy, a forecast that leans high by a share b raises the target level by b x weekly demand x (lead time + 1). That stock is bought, stored and insured, and no service metric notices it.

LiveWhy the override rule belongs in the business case3 min▶

On the "Claim + upward overrides" rung, the vendor's 30 percent cut is real, and planners add 10 percent to every forecast because they do not yet trust it. Cash released falls from £1,042,179 to £82,179. Net value a year falls from £354,727 to £114,727, and payback stretches from 5.1 to 15.7 months. The break-even error cut rises to 24.3 percent.

The research behind session 5 is why this is not a cartoon. Fildes and colleagues report, from the abstract of their 2009 study of more than 60,000 forecasts, that upward adjustments were much less likely to improve accuracy than downward ones. Dietvorst and colleagues found that people use an imperfect algorithm more readily when they can modify it slightly. Put together: allow small, logged, bounded overrides, and price the habit you expect.

Self-studyWhat the simulator leaves out3 min▶
  • Bias in the tool itself. Session 1's learned model ran 17.3 percent short. The simulator assumes the error spread shrinks in proportion to WAPE and that the tool is unbiased. A tool that leans low releases cash and quietly costs service.
  • Cycle stock and markdowns. Only safety stock and units short are priced. A better forecast can also cut end-of-season markdowns, which can be larger. Add that line only with your own markdown data.
  • Planner time. Hours saved are real and are left out, because they are rarely released as cash. If they are, add them; if they are not, do not count them.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · At Northwick's settings the vendor's 30 percent cut nets £354,727 a year and the measured 2.7 percent nets -£196,011. What should the playbook ask the vendor for?

The break-even turns a disputed claim into a test. Neither number has to be taken on trust: the pilot either clears 12.4 percent on Northwick's data or it does not.

2 · Restricting the tool to the six cells where it wins raises the break-even from 12.4 to 22.9 percent. Why?

The licence does not shrink with coverage. Planning fewer SKUs means less safety stock for the cut to release, so the cut needed to pay the same fee rises.

3 · With the vendor's cut and a 10 percent upward override on every forecast, cash released falls from £1,042,179 to £82,179. Where did the rest go?

A forecast that leans high by 10 percent raises the target level by 10 percent of weekly demand times lead time plus one. It is stock on a shelf, not service anyone measured.
