Learn AI + Supply Chain with Phoebe

Six sessions, 45 minutes each, for ops, supply chain and planning leads being sold an AI forecasting or planning tool, and for the planners and analysts who will run it. One question runs through all six: the vendor says the forecast is 30 percent better, so how much stock, service and cash does that actually move, and where does a spreadsheet still win? You run five forecasters on 45 real retail SKUs in your browser, turn the error into stock and allocation rules, watch a real bullwhip grow and shrink, and price the tool on a live bench. No code, no vendor account.

The six sessions 🚚 · one planning playbook, built across all of them

Bring a real planning question if you have one: a vendor proposal, a forecast you run, a stock policy you inherited. Every session adds one section to Northwick's AI planning playbook (a constructed homeware retailer), and you can write yours beside it.

🎯 Session 1 · easy

Every forecast is wrong: budget the error

Bias vs spread, ABC/XYZ, and five forecasters on 45 real SKUs. The learned model beats the best spreadsheet by 2.7 percent and runs 17.3 percent short.

▶ Start here playground 📦 Session 2 · medium

From forecast to stock: safety stock and service level

z x sigma x sqrt(lead time), cycle service vs fill rate, and the newsvendor. A policy that promised 98 percent on A items delivers 97.4 on weeks it never saw.

stock policy ⚖️ Session 3 · medium

Allocate scarce stock: rules before optimisers

1,200 lanterns, 1,600 wanted, four rules. One gives online nothing, one gives the most margin, and an optimiser cannot choose between them for you.

halfway scorecard 🌊 Session 4 · hands-on

Shocks and the bullwhip

104 real weeks through a three-tier chain of sensible rules: the supplier sees 87.6 times the swing. Sharing sell-through brings it to 16.5.

bullwhip lever 🧑‍💼 Session 5 · hands-on

When the planner is right: overrides and trust

Forecast value added, what 60,000 adjusted forecasts reportedly showed about upward overrides, and the unresolved argument over trusting algorithms.

contested 🧮 Session 6 · hardest

The business case: price it on the bench

Error cut to safety stock, cash and units short, minus the licence. The claim pays, the measurement does not, and the break-even is 12.4 percent.
